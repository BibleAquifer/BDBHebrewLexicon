## ו (id: BDB02500)

ו, *Wāw*, sixth letter; in modern Heb. \= numeral 6; no evidence of this usage in OT times.



## וְ (id: BDB02501)

וְ, וּ, וָ **demonstr. adv.** and **conj.** so, then, and (Ph. Moab. Aram. ו; Ar. وَ; Eth. ወ፡ As. *u* DlW (but between vbs. the usu. connective is the enclitic *ma* KAT2. 508, Dl:§§ 82, 150\). The use in Heb. shews that orig. וְ was not a merely copulative conj., but that it possessed a *demonstrative* force (cf. Dr§ 122\), which is often perceptible as such, though in other cases, from its being applied to denote mere juxtaposition or connexion \[cf. Gk. τε, and Lat. *tum…tum* \= ‘ both…and, ’ both properly demonstratives: As. *ma* has also a demonstr. force, Dl:§ 79], it is less palpable)—וְ before shwa’ mobile or a labial is vocalized וּ; often before a tone\-syll., when the word itself has a disj. accent, וָ (as :וָמַֽתִּי [Gn 19:19](https://ref.ly/Gen19:19); וָמֵ֭תוּ [33:13](https://ref.ly/Gen33:13); וָמֵָת [44:9](https://ref.ly/Gen44:9); :וָחָֽיָה [Ex 1:16](https://ref.ly/Exod1:16); וָ\+ימָּה [Jos 15:46](https://ref.ly/Josh15:46); וָ֔ל֕אֹ [2 S 13:26](https://ref.ly/2Sam13:26); וָאָ֭רֶץ [Is 26:19](https://ref.ly/Isa26:19); וָקָ֔מוּ [49:7](https://ref.ly/Isa49:7); :וָחָֽתָּה [Je 48:1](https://ref.ly/Jer48:1); וָרָֹע [Psalm 10:15](https://ref.ly/Ps10:15)); esp. when it forms with the preceding word a *pair,* whether of parallel or opposed ideas (as כֹּה וָכֹה [Ex 2:12](https://ref.ly/Exod2:12), מִי וָמִ֭י [10:8](https://ref.ly/Exod10:8), אֶבֶן וָאָבֶן [Dt 25:13](https://ref.ly/Deut25:13); דֹּר וָדָֹר [32:7](https://ref.ly/Deut32:7) \+; תֹּהוּ וָבֹ֔הוּ [Gn 1:2](https://ref.ly/Gen1:2), בְּהֵמָה וָרֶמֶש [1:24](https://ref.ly/Gen1:24), :טוֺב וָרָֽע [2:19](https://ref.ly/Gen2:19), נָע וָנָ֭ד [4:12](https://ref.ly/Gen4:12), וְקַיִץ וָחֶֹרֶף וְיוֺם וָלָ֭יְלָה [8:22](https://ref.ly/Gen8:22), :קַח וָלֵֽח [12:19](https://ref.ly/Gen12:19), :שָׁמַיִם וָאָֽרֶץ [14:19](https://ref.ly/Gen14:19), אֲנִי וָאָתָּה [31:44](https://ref.ly/Gen31:44), אֲנִי וָהָוּא [41:11](https://ref.ly/Gen41:11), עִבְרוּ וָשׁ֝וּבוּ [Ex 32:27](https://ref.ly/Exod32:27), אַתָּה וָהֵֶם [Nu 16:16](https://ref.ly/Num16:16), אֱלֽהִים וָמֶָלָךְ [1 K 21:13](https://ref.ly/1Kgs21:13), נִין וָנֶ֭כֶד [Is 14:22](https://ref.ly/Isa14:22), שֹׁד וָשֶׁ֭בֶר [59:7](https://ref.ly/Isa59:7); and often: v. more fully Bö§ 600\); occas. also with a conj. accent (as :וָשַחי לְעֹלָֽם [Gn 3:22](https://ref.ly/Gen3:22) cf. [Lv 18:5](https://ref.ly/Lev18:5), וָמַ֣תְנוּ שָׁם [2 K 7:4](https://ref.ly/2Kgs7:4), וָאָ֣רֶץ חֲדָשָׁה [Is 65:17](https://ref.ly/Isa65:17), [Pr 25:3](https://ref.ly/Prov25:3), :שֹׁ֭ד וָשֶשׁבֶר גָּדֽוֺל [Je 48:3](https://ref.ly/Jer48:3), cf. [32:29](https://ref.ly/Jer32:29)).—וְ is used very freely and widely in Heb., but also with much delicacy, to express relations and shades of meaning which Western languages would usu. indicate by distinct particles. But in Heb. particles such as אוֺ, אָז, אַךְ, אָכֵן, אוּלָם, בַּעֲבוּר, לְמַעַן, לָכֵן, etc., were reserved for cases in which special emph. or distinctness was desired: their frequent use was felt instinctively to be inconsistent with the lightness and grace of movement which the Hebrew ear loved; and thus in AV, RV, words like *or, then, but, notwithstanding, howbeit, so, thus, therefore, that,* constantly appear, where the Heb. has simply וְ.
